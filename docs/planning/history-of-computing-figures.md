# History of Computing — Chronological Timeline of Foundational Figures

Chronological list (by date of major contribution), from 17th-century calculating mechanisms to contemporary deep learning.

---

**1642 — Blaise Pascal**
The Pascaline, one of the first mechanical calculating machines.

**1673 — Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz**
Theorizes the binary system; designs a calculating machine capable of all four operations. Proposes the idea that logical reasoning could be "computed."

**1804 — Joseph Marie Jacquard**
The punched-card loom: the first "programmable memory" in history, directly inspiring Babbage.

**1830s — Charles Babbage**
The Difference Engine, then the Analytical Engine: the first architecture of a programmable computer (memory, arithmetic unit, control via punched cards), never built in his lifetime.

**1843 — Ada Lovelace**
Writes what is considered the first computer program (an algorithm to compute Bernoulli numbers on the Analytical Engine); anticipates that machines could manipulate symbols, not just numbers.

**1854 — George Boole**
*The Laws of Thought*: Boolean algebra, the logical language behind all future digital circuits.

**1890 — Herman Hollerith**
Punched-card machine for the U.S. census; his company would become IBM. The link between Jacquard/Babbage and the industrialization of data processing.

**1936 — Alan Turing**
*On Computable Numbers*: the Turing machine, a theoretical model of all possible computation, founding computability theory. Contributes to codebreaking at Bletchley Park; lays the groundwork for AI (the Turing test).

**1937 — Claude Shannon**
Master's thesis showing that Boolean algebra can be implemented with electrical relay circuits: the direct bridge between logic and machines.

**1937 — Vannevar Bush**
Essay *As We May Think* (published 1945), anticipates hypertext and augmented-memory systems; directs U.S. scientific research during the war. Would directly inspire Engelbart and Ted Nelson.

**1937-42 — John Atanasoff and Clifford Berry**
The Atanasoff-Berry Computer, one of the first to use electronics and binary arithmetic.

**1941 — Konrad Zuse**
The Z3, the first functional programmable computer in the world; also invents Plankalkül, one of the first high-level languages.

**1943-45 — Katherine Johnson, Dorothy Vaughan, Mary Jackson**
"Human computers" at NACA/NASA, calculating orbital trajectories by hand and then on early computers — the transition from human to machine computation.

**1944 — Howard Aiken**
The Harvard Mark I, built with IBM.

**1945 — J. Presper Eckert and John Mauchly**
The ENIAC, the first large-scale general-purpose electronic computer.

**1945 — The ENIAC programmers** (Kay McNulty, Jean Bartik, Betty Holberton, Marlyn Meltzer, Ruth Teitelbaum, Frances Spence)
The first programmers in history, long left out of the official record.

**1945 — John von Neumann**
*First Draft of a Report on the EDVAC*: the "von Neumann" architecture (a single memory for program and data), still the basis of computers today.

**1947 — William Shockley, John Bardeen, Walter Brattain**
Invention of the transistor at Bell Labs, replacing vacuum tubes.

**1948 — Claude Shannon**
Founds information theory.

**1948 — Norbert Wiener**
Founds cybernetics, the study of control and feedback systems.

**1949 — Jay Forrester**
Invents magnetic-core memory at MIT for the Whirlwind project: the dominant form of RAM until semiconductors in the 1970s.

**1949-51 — Sergei Lebedev**
Builds the MESM in Kiev, the first electronic stored-program computer in the Soviet Union; goes on to found an entire school of Soviet computing (BESM, M-20), a parallel and little-known history to that of the West.

**1952 — Grace Hopper**
Develops the first compiler; popularizes English-like programming languages; co-designs COBOL. Often credited with popularizing the term "bug."

**1955 — An Wang**
Files a key patent on magnetic-core memory; founds Wang Laboratories.

**1956 — John McCarthy**
Coins the term "artificial intelligence" at the Dartmouth Conference.

**1957 — John Backus**
Leads the creation of Fortran, the first widely adopted high-level language; invents BNF notation to describe grammars.

**1958 — Jack Kilby and Robert Noyce**
Independently invent the integrated circuit, enabling miniaturization.

**1958 — John McCarthy**
Creates LISP.

**1959 — Edsger Dijkstra**
The shortest-path algorithm.

**1960 — C.A.R. Hoare**
Invents Quicksort.

**1960 — Charles Bachman**
Creates IDS (Integrated Data Store), the first major database management system (the "network" model) — a direct predecessor that Codd's relational model would later supersede. Turing Award 1973.

**1962 — J.C.R. Licklider**
The "Man-Computer Symbiosis" memo and his vision of an "intergalactic computer network"; directs ARPA funding that would lead to ARPANET.

**1963 — Ivan Sutherland**
Sketchpad, the forerunner of interactive computer graphics and CAD.

**1963 — Peter Naur**
Formalizes, with Backus, the BNF notation while editing the ALGOL 60 report; a key figure in the formal rigor of programming languages.

**1964 — Douglas Engelbart**
Invents the mouse; will present it all in 1968 ("The Mother of All Demos"): hypertext, windows, real-time collaborative editing.

**1965 — Ted Nelson**
Coins the term "hypertext"; the Xanadu project, a direct influence on the Web.

**1965 — Gordon Moore**
Formulates "Moore's Law" on the doubling of transistor density.

**1965 — Kristen Nygaard and Ole-Johan Dahl**
Create Simula, the first object-oriented language.

**1966 — Robert Taylor**
Director of the IPTO office at ARPA, initiates and funds the project that becomes ARPANET; later directs Xerox PARC.

**1967 — Fred Brooks**
Leads the development of OS/360 at IBM; will draw on it for *The Mythical Man-Month* (1975), a foundational text of software engineering.

**1968 — Edsger Dijkstra**
*Go To Statement Considered Harmful*, structured programming.

**1968 — Douglas McIlroy**
Invents the Unix pipe (`|`) concept; co-architect, alongside Thompson and Ritchie, of the Unix philosophy ("do one thing and do it well").

**1968 — Donald Knuth**
*The Art of Computer Programming*, founding the rigorous analysis of algorithms and their complexity; also creates TeX.

**1968 — Alan Kay**
Develops Smalltalk and the concept of the modern graphical interface at Xerox PARC; coins "The best way to predict the future is to invent it."

**1969 — Ken Thompson and Dennis Ritchie**
Create Unix at Bell Labs.

**1969 — C.A.R. Hoare**
Hoare logic, a foundation of formal program verification.

**1969 — Margaret Hamilton**
Leads the development of the Apollo program's flight software; popularizes the term "software engineering."

**1970 — Edgar F. Codd**
Invents the relational model, the foundation of SQL and nearly all modern databases.

**1970 — Niklaus Wirth**
Creates Pascal, then Modula and Oberon; coins "Algorithms + Data Structures = Programs."

**1971 — Federico Faggin**
Designs the Intel 4004 microprocessor, making the personal computer possible.

**1972 — Dennis Ritchie**
Creates the C language to rewrite Unix.

**1972 — Karen Spärck Jones**
Invents the concept of inverse document frequency (IDF), the mathematical foundation of modern search engines and much of NLP.

**1973 — Robert Metcalfe**
Invents Ethernet at Xerox PARC, still the dominant local-network standard today.

**1973 — Butler Lampson and Chuck Thacker**
Design the Xerox Alto, the first computer with an integrated graphical interface, mouse, and network — the true prototype of the modern PC.

**1974 — Vint Cerf and Bob Kahn**
Design TCP/IP, the fundamental protocol of the Internet.

**1974 — Gary Kildall**
Creates CP/M, the dominant operating system for microcomputers before MS-DOS.

**1975 — Bill Gates and Paul Allen**
Write a BASIC interpreter for the Altair 8800, found Microsoft.

**1976 — Steve Wozniak**
Designs the Apple I and Apple II, democratizing the personal computer alongside Steve Jobs.

**1976 — Whitfield Diffie and Martin Hellman**
Diffie-Hellman key exchange, founding public-key cryptography.

**1977 — Ron Rivest, Adi Shamir, Leonard Adleman**
Create RSA.

**1978 — Barbara Liskov**
Major contributions to object-oriented programming and data abstraction (the Liskov substitution principle).

**1978 — Leslie Lamport**
Logical clocks in distributed systems; later the Paxos algorithm and LaTeX.

**1979 — Dan Bricklin and Bob Frankston**
Create VisiCalc, the first spreadsheet — the killer app that justified buying a personal computer for business use.

**1982-85 — Shafi Goldwasser and Silvio Micali**
Found modern theoretical cryptography: zero-knowledge proofs, semantic security. Turing Award 2012.

**1983 — Bjarne Stroustrup**
Creates C++.

**1983 — Richard Stallman**
The GNU Project, the Free Software Foundation, the GPL license: founds the free software movement.

**1985 — Sophie Wilson**
Designs the ARM instruction set (with Steve Furber), now present in nearly every smartphone in the world; also wrote BBC BASIC.

**1989-91 — Tim Berners-Lee**
Invents the World Wide Web: HTTP, HTML, URL.

**1991 — Linus Torvalds**
Creates the Linux kernel.

**1991 — Guido van Rossum**
Creates Python.

**1991 — Phil Zimmermann**
Creates PGP, bringing strong encryption to the general public.

**1993 — Marc Andreessen and Eric Bina**
Mosaic, the first mainstream web browser, triggers the explosion of the Web.

**1996-98 — Larry Page and Sergey Brin**
Invent the PageRank algorithm, found Google.

**2005 — Linus Torvalds**
Creates Git.

**2000s-2010s — Brendan Gregg**
Modern systems observability, flame graphs, eBPF.

**2006 — Geoffrey Hinton, Yann LeCun, Yoshua Bengio**
Revive and lay the foundations of contemporary deep learning (backpropagation, convolutional networks). Turing Award 2018.

**2008 — Satoshi Nakamoto** (identity unknown)
Publishes the Bitcoin white paper, invents blockchain.

---

*Areas not covered in depth, worth digging into further: Japanese computing (the Fifth Generation Computer project, NEC, Fujitsu), other telecom figures, and Soviet/Eastern European computing beyond Lebedev.*
