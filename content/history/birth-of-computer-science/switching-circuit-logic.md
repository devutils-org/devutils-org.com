## The problem

Relay networks were used to control equipment and connect paths. Their designers needed ways to reason about behavior and to find circuits satisfying a specification. Shannon’s paper treats both analysis — finding what a network does — and synthesis — finding a network that does what is required.

## The connection

The paper draws its notation from symbolic logic and applies mathematical reasoning to switch networks. Equivalent expressions can describe alternative arrangements. This makes algebra useful for comparing designs rather than relying entirely on an individual drawing.

Our [Boolean-algebra article](/library/digital-logic/boolean-algebra/) introduces a small conducting-path model. It is a modern teaching explanation, not a reproduction of Shannon’s notation or all of the paper’s methods.

## Why two dates appear

MIT’s historical overview places the thesis work in 1937. The paper’s own publication note identifies AIEE Transactions, volume 57, in 1938. This entry therefore uses the year range 1937–1938 and distinguishes thesis work from publication; it does not assign a single exact day to the whole development. MIT News separately dates the award of Shannon’s SM and PhD degrees to 1940. The work, the paper and the degree award are different milestones.

## Significance and limits

This is a documented connection between symbolic reasoning and physical switching networks. It does not establish that Shannon invented logic, relays or every form of digital computation. Read the [profile](/figures/claude-shannon/) for the separate connection to his later communication work.

The timeline is intentionally selective: this is the first sourced event in this site’s dataset, not the beginning of the history of computing.
