## A publication about a proposed machine

In 1843 Ada Lovelace collaborated with Babbage on an account of the Analytical Engine, adding notes to a description by Luigi Menabrea. Oxford's manuscript-based exhibit includes a diagram showing the steps of a Bernoulli-number calculation. The complete engine was not built, so the publication is evidence of a described computation, not a record of a completed engine running it.

## What the date means

The year marks the explanatory publication, not the design of every part, the origin of all algorithms or an uncontested first-program milestone. Oxford explicitly cautions against a simple first-computer-programme label. The chapter on [mechanical computing](/history/mechanical-computing/) places the work beside calculators, card control and difference engines.

## Read the evidence carefully

A later reconstruction or modern simulation can test aspects of a design under specified conditions. It cannot retrospectively turn the published diagram into a nineteenth-century execution report. This entry makes no hardware performance claim.
